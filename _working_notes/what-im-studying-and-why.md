I've never been good at explaining what I do. The work happens out of sight, and the words for it sound like a foreign language. Every time someone asks what I'm going to school for, my answer turns into a ten-minute ramble.

So I wrote it down.

This page is for my family and friends. No jargon. I promise.

## The short version

I'm learning how to build and run the systems that keep websites and apps online.

The degree is a Bachelor of Science in Cloud and Network Engineering from Western Governors University (WGU). I'm on the Amazon Web Services track.

## How I got here

My first job in tech was Tier 3 support for AT&T. After that I worked help desk at an IT company in Sparks, and before long I was building websites. That turned into Xaicode, the web development business I ran for about eight years.

At Xaicode I managed developers in different countries and moved whole online stores from one platform to another. The part I liked most was the work behind the scenes. Some clients had sites with heavy traffic, and I had to figure out how to keep them fast and online. I taught myself servers through a lot of late nights, long before AI tools could help. Eventually I ran my own server to host sites for smaller clients.

I liked that work more than anything else I did. So I went back to school for it.

## What cloud and network engineering means

### Networking

A network is how computers talk to each other. Think of it like the roads in a city. When you send a text or open a website, information travels across roads and intersections to get where it's going. Network engineers design those roads and fix them when traffic backs up.

### The cloud

"The cloud" is other people's computers. A company can buy a room full of servers and hire people to maintain them. Or it can rent computing power from a big provider and pay for what it uses. It's like paying an electric bill instead of running your own power plant.

### AWS

Amazon Web Services (AWS) is one of the biggest of those providers. A lot of the apps you use every day run on AWS. You'd never know it.

## What I can do today

I wired my own house. I ran network cable through the walls to a closet and set up the equipment that connects everything. I also run a small Linux lab at home. Linux is the operating system most servers run on, so that's where I practice what I learn in class.

Here's where my certifications stand:

- **ITIL 4 Foundation (2026):** How IT teams organize their work so problems get fixed the same way every time.
- **LPI Linux Essentials (2026):** Proof that I know my way around Linux.
- **CompTIA A+:** The standard entry-level IT certification. I passed the first of two exams. The second one is on October 9.
- **CompTIA Network+:** Next on the list.

## Where I'm headed

1. **Finish the degree and certifications.** Expected graduation: December 2027.
2. **Land an entry-level cloud job.** Reno has a growing data center industry, and I want in. Long term, I'd love to work somewhere like GitHub or Cloudflare.
3. **Move into DevOps.** DevOps engineers build the systems that let software teams release updates fast and safely. They connect the people who write code with the people who run the servers.
4. **Get a master's degree** in software engineering, with a DevOps focus.
5. **Stay hands-on.** Management isn't the goal. I'd rather be architecting systems, deploying them, and automating the boring parts.

- **Send me job leads.** Networking and cloud roles around Reno and Sparks, plus IT and help desk jobs. Data centers and companies that use AWS are at the top of my list. Remote works too.
- **Introduce me.** If you know someone who works in IT, I'd love 15 minutes to hear how they got there.

## Questions people ask me

### Is this coding?

Some. I write scripts to automate tasks and set up systems. I don't build apps. Most of my time goes to setting systems up and fixing them when they break.

### Why go back to school?

I've spent years in tech, mostly on the business and project side. I learned a lot on my own, but a lot of the jobs I want ask for a degree and certifications. Soon I'll have both.

### Is WGU a real school?

Yes. It's an accredited, nonprofit online university. You don't sit through a semester. You move through each course as soon as you prove you know the material.
